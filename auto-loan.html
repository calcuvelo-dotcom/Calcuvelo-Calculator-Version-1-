What is the Auto Loan Calculator?

The sticker price is rarely the amount you finance. By the time sales tax, dealer fees and any trade-in are settled, the loan balance can be several thousand dollars different from the price on the windshield, and the advertised “$399 a month” depends heavily on how long the loan runs. This calculator rebuilds the real numbers so you can compare a deal on total cost instead of on the monthly payment alone.

It is most useful at the dealership-adjacent stage: you have a price in mind, a quote or two on the interest rate, and you want to know what it will actually cost.

Using each input

  • Vehicle price. The negotiated price before tax and fees.
  • Down payment and trade-in. Both reduce the amount borrowed. In many states the trade-in also reduces the amount that is taxed; the calculator assumes it does, so check your state’s rule.
  • Sales tax %. Your combined state and local rate. Some states tax at registration, some at the dealership.
  • Fees. Documentation, title and registration. Ask for these in writing before you agree on a price.
  • Term and APR. Use the rate from a real offer. A pre-approval from a bank or credit union is a good benchmark to hold against the dealer’s financing.

How does the underlying math work?

First the amount financed is assembled, then the standard installment-loan formula turns it into a payment.

financed = price + sales tax + fees − down payment − trade-in
payment = financed × i × (1 + i)n ÷ [ (1 + i)n − 1 ]

Here i is the APR divided by 12 and n the number of months. Total interest is the payment times n, minus the amount financed. Note that the payment formula is the same for every installment loan, which is why the same math appears in the Mortgage Calculator.

Worked example: a $32,000 car, 60 months versus 84

Price $32,000, down payment $3,000, no trade-in, 6% sales tax ($1,920), fees $600, APR 7.2%. The amount financed is 32,000 + 1,920 + 600 − 3,000 = $31,520.

  • 60 months: payment about $627.11, total interest about $6,107, total cost of the car (including the down payment) about $40,627.
  • 84 months: payment about $478.81, total interest about $8,700, total cost about $43,220.

Stretching the loan by two years lowers the payment by roughly $148 a month but adds about $2,593 in interest. The longer loan is also where owners most often end up “upside-down” (owing more than the car is worth), because the car depreciates faster than a slow-amortizing loan is paid down.

Real-world practical application

Use it to put two financing offers side by side — say, 5.9% from a credit union versus 7.4% from the dealer — and see the dollar difference over the term, not just the rate gap. It also helps you test how much a larger down payment or a longer-lasting trade-in is really worth, and whether the dealer’s “add the extended warranty to the loan” suggestion is as cheap per month as it sounds once it is financed for six years.

Limits worth knowing

The calculator assumes a simple-interest loan with equal monthly payments and does not model rebates that apply only to certain financing, negative equity rolled in from a previous car, or gap insurance. Because several fees and tax treatments vary by state, treat the output as a close estimate and confirm every line with the dealer’s buyer’s order.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should a car loan be?

Many consumer-finance writers suggest keeping the term to 60 months or less so the loan is paid off while the car still has value. A common guideline is the “20/4/10” rule: 20% down, a loan of no more than four years, and total transportation costs under 10% of gross income.

Is it better to finance through the dealer or a bank?

Get a pre-approval from a bank or credit union first. Dealers sometimes beat it, especially with manufacturer promotional rates, and sometimes mark the rate up. Having a benchmark in hand lets you compare honestly.

Does a trade-in reduce sales tax?

In many states, yes: tax is charged only on the difference between the price and the trade-in value. Some states do not give this credit, so verify locally.

Why is my monthly payment higher than the advertised one?

Advertised payments often assume a large down payment, a long term and excellent credit, and they exclude tax and fees. Enter your real numbers to see the payment you would actually owe.

Can I pay a car loan off early?

Most auto loans allow it, but check for a prepayment penalty. The Payoff vs. Interest Calculator shows how much interest an early payoff would avoid.
