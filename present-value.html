How to Use This Calculator

  1. Pick what to solve for. "Grow a lump sum → FV" turns a starting amount into a future value. "Discount a future sum → PV" tells you what a future target is worth today.
  2. Enter the amount — a starting balance in FV mode, or the future target in PV mode.
  3. Add a payment per period if money is added (FV) or set aside (PV) every period. Leave it at 0 for a pure lump-sum calculation.
  4. Enter the annual rate, the number of periods, and the compounding frequency.
  5. Read the results: the solved value, your total contributions, and the portion that is interest or growth. The chart traces the running value over every period.

The Formula Explained

Future value of a present amount plus an ordinary annuity of payments:

FV = PV (1 + i)ⁿ + PMT × [ ((1 + i)ⁿ − 1) ÷ i ]

Rearranged to solve for the present value that reaches a future target:

PV = ( FV − PMT × [ ((1 + i)ⁿ − 1) ÷ i ] ) ÷ (1 + i)ⁿ
  • i — rate per period = annual rate ÷ compounding frequency
  • n — total number of periods
  • PMT — payment made at the end of each period

Frequently Asked Questions

What discount rate should I use?

Use your opportunity cost — the return available on a comparable alternative. For personal decisions that is often the expected return of a diversified portfolio (roughly 6–8%); a business usually uses its weighted average cost of capital. A higher rate makes future dollars worth less today.

What is the difference between present value and net present value?

Present value discounts one future amount or a stream of equal payments to today. Net present value (NPV) does this for an irregular series of cash flows and subtracts the upfront cost, producing one number that is positive when a project adds value.

Why is a dollar today worth more than a dollar tomorrow?

A dollar today can be invested and earn a return, inflation eats into the buying power of future dollars, and any promised future payment carries some risk of not arriving. Those three forces give money its time value.
