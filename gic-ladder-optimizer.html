      What is the GIC & Cash Ladder Yield Optimizer?

      The Canadian Cash & GIC Ladder Yield Optimizer compares the after-tax return of keeping your emergency fund or short-term savings in a high-interest savings account (HISA) against spreading it across GICs with staggered maturities — a GIC ladder. It accounts for promotional HISA rates that expire, the exact rate each GIC rung pays, your marginal tax rate, and whether the money sits inside a TFSA, RRSP, or a regular taxable account, then reports which approach actually leaves you with more money and how liquid it stays along the way.

      How does the underlying math work?

      Behind the scenes, the calculator treats each GIC rung and the HISA balance as its own cash-flow schedule. A HISA earns its promotional rate compounded monthly for the promo window, then reverts to the base rate for the rest of your horizon; each GIC rung compounds annually at its posted term rate until maturity, and maturing rungs are assumed reinvested to keep filling the time you have left. Outside a registered account, every dollar of interest is reduced by your marginal tax rate before it is added back to the running balance. The “effective after-tax yield” shown in your results is not a simple average of the posted rates — it is the single constant annual rate that would have grown your actual deposit schedule, after tax, to the same ending balance, weighted by how much money was earning interest and for how long (a money-weighted return).

      Real-world practical application

      Savers use this tool to decide whether locking part of an emergency fund into a 1- or 2-year GIC rung is worth the lost liquidity once a promotional HISA rate expires, to see how much a TFSA or RRSP wrapper is worth in after-tax dollars compared with a taxable account, and to build a ladder that releases a rung every few months so cash keeps becoming available without ever needing to break a long-term GIC early. It is also useful for checking CDIC coverage limits before splitting a large balance across institutions.

      Frequently Asked Questions

      Is my money safe in a GIC ladder?

      Eligible deposits at CDIC member institutions are insured up to $100,000 per depositor, per insured category, per institution. Splitting a ladder across more than one institution, or across registered and non-registered accounts, can extend that protection further. Always confirm an institution's CDIC membership directly before depositing.

      What happens if I need to withdraw a GIC early?

      Most non-redeemable GICs do not allow early withdrawal without forfeiting most or all of the accrued interest, which is why a ladder staggers maturities instead of locking everything into one long term. A HISA or a short 30–90 day cashable GIC rung is typically kept alongside the ladder to cover true emergencies.

      Why does a TFSA or RRSP change the result so much?

      Interest earned inside a TFSA or RRSP is not taxed in the year it is earned, so the full posted rate compounds every year. Outside a registered account, your marginal tax rate takes a bite out of every interest payment before it can compound, and that gap widens the longer the ladder runs.

      How often should I rebuild the ladder?

      As each rung matures, reinvest it into a new rung at the far end of your ladder — for example, a new 5-year GIC — to keep the staggered schedule going, or redirect it to whichever option, HISA or GIC, currently offers the better effective after-tax yield for your remaining time horizon.
